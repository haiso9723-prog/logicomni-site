Synthetic demonstration

See the proposed brief
before discussing it.

This is a hand-prepared, fictional example for research discussion. It is not customer work. It is not a product demonstration or evidence that every change would be detected. The source labels and project details below were invented solely to show the proposed format.

Fictional scenario

One late addendum.
Three review paths.

A small electrical contractor is preparing a bid for a fictional community-center renovation. “Addendum 03” arrives two days before submission.

Illustrative sources: revised sheet E-201, lighting-control schedule LCS-2, specification section 26 28 16, and revised bid form BF-2.

Proposed job: organize each stated change, point to its fictional source, identify the review area it may affect, and keep open questions visible for a human estimator.

Boundary: the brief does not price the work, approve a bid, interpret a contract, or replace review of the issued documents.

01 / EQUIPMENT

Feeder protection changed.

Source reference: revised E-201, note 7; fictional section 26 28 16, paragraph 2.3.

Stated change: the main feeder breaker is shown as 400 A instead of 225 A.

Review impact: estimator to recheck equipment quote, feeder conductors, raceway and supplier lead-time assumptions. Electrical engineer confirmation remains an open question.

02 / CONTROLS

Additional sensor locations listed.

Source reference: fictional schedule LCS-2, rooms 110–116.

Stated change: seven rooms now list ceiling occupancy sensors.

Review impact: estimator to verify device count, control wiring and whether the current controls quotation includes the added rooms. Drawing-to-schedule consistency remains unresolved.

03 / SUBMISSION

Bid acknowledgement revised.

Source reference: fictional bid form BF-2, page 1.

Stated change: the addendum acknowledgement and one alternate line were revised.

Review impact: bid lead to replace the prior form, acknowledge Addendum 03 and confirm the alternate is carried into the final review. This is an administrative check, not legal advice.

Human review remains central

The brief should expose
what is still unknown.

Does the equipment supplier confirm the revised rating and delivery date?

Are the seven sensor locations additions, clarifications or replacements?

Did any drawing, specification or bid-form reference conflict with another issued source?

Who owns each final check, and when was it completed?
