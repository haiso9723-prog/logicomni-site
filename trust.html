Trust principles

Trust starts with clear claims and visible limits.

The public standard is simple: say what is known, show where it came from and keep conclusions proportional to the evidence.

Six practical principles

Clear scope

State the question, intended outcome, exclusions and stopping conditions.

Source transparency

Make the basis for important claims traceable and reviewable.

Facts before interpretation

Separate observations from assumptions, judgments and open questions.

Data minimization

Request only what the stated purpose needs and define handling before receipt.

Visible limitations

Surface contradictory signals, missing evidence and material uncertainty.

Proportionate conclusions

Avoid stronger claims or commitments than the evidence supports.

Claims boundary

These are working principles, not a certification, independent audit, confidentiality guarantee, legal conclusion or assurance of outcomes.
